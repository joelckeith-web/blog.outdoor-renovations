# [ASP] Client Testimonial — Capture Script & Ad Format Guide

Purpose: run a 45–60 minute recorded call with a happy [ASP] client and walk away with
enough self-contained soundbites to cut **6–8 paid social assets** plus a website testimonial —
without needing a second session.

Formats this script is engineered to feed:

| Format | Spec | Layout |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical split | 1080×1920 (9:16) | Client top pane, interviewer bottom pane |
| Stat card | 1080×1350 (4:5) | Client full-frame, animated stat overlays |
| Full-frame quote | 1080×1920 (9:16) | Client full-frame, burned captions |
| Website | 1920×1080 (16:9) | Standard two-shot or single |

---

## 1. Before the call

### Send the client this, 48 hours ahead

> Hey [Name] — excited for this. Three things to make you look great:
>
> 1. **Sit facing a window or a lamp**, never with a window behind you.
> 2. **Wired earbuds or a headset mic.** Laptop mics kill it.
> 3. **Hardwired ethernet if you have it**, otherwise sit next to the router.
>
> Sit so your **head is centered and you're framed from mid-chest up**, with a
> little space above your head. Camera at eye level — stack of books under the laptop.
> Wear a solid color, no tight stripes or logos-on-logos.
>
> No prep needed. I'll ask you about before/after and we'll talk numbers.
> If it helps, have your [dashboard / P&L / booking calendar] open so you can
> pull an exact figure if I ask. Don't script anything — I want you, not a speech.

### Framing notes that make the split-screen work

The vertical split crops each source frame hard. Frame both parties for a **near-square
crop**, not a widescreen one:

- **Client (top pane, 1080×960):** medium-close, chest up, **centered**, ~10% headroom.
  If they're framed too wide, the crop leaves them tiny in the top pane and there's no
  fixing it in post.
- **You (bottom pane, 1080×960 spanning y=960–1920):** frame yourself **low in your own
  pane** so your face lands around y=1100–1400. Everything below y≈1500 is covered by
  Reels/Stories UI — likes, caption, CTA, profile row. If you center yourself in the
  bottom pane, the platform covers your chin.
- **Prefer a 55/45 split** (client 0–1050, you 1050–1920) over a true 50/50. You get more
  face on the client, and your pane's usable band gets bigger. Visually it still reads
  as a split.
- **For the 4:5 stat cut:** ask the client to sit **slightly off-center toward screen-left**
  so the right third stays clean for animated stat cards. If they're dead-center, your
  stat overlays land on their face.

### Recording setup

- **Record local tracks, separately.** Riverside, Zoom local recording with "separate
  audio files for each participant," or equivalent. Never rely on the cloud composite —
  you cannot build a split screen from a pre-baked gallery view.
- **1080p minimum per participant.** 4K on the client's side if their machine can hold it —
  the vertical crop throws away most of the pixels.
- **Record 30 seconds of room tone** on both sides before you start talking.
- **Clap once** at the top for a sync point if the tracks ever drift.

## 2. Run of show

| Time | Block | What you're harvesting |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Warm-up + ground rules (off the record) | Comfort, the restate-the-question habit |
| 0:05–0:08 | ID stamp | Lower-third text, cold open |
| 0:08–0:18 | The Before | Hook fuel, pain that mirrors your cold audience |
| 0:18–0:25 | The Hesitation | Objection-handling ad |
| 0:25–0:32 | The Switch | Risk reversal, "it was easier than I thought" |
| 0:32–0:42 | The Numbers | Stat pops for the 4:5 |
| 0:42–0:48 | The Payoff | The emotional line that carries the whole ad |
| 0:48–0:53 | Soundbite harvest | Punchy one-liners, direct-to-camera CTA |
| 0:53–1:00 | Silent captures + admin | Reaction beds, room tone, consent, assets |

## 3. The warm-up (say this verbatim, before you start asking real questions)

> Two housekeeping things and then we'll just talk.
>
> **One:** answer in complete sentences that swallow my question. So if I ask "what was
> the hardest part," don't say "the leads." Say "the hardest part was the leads." You'll
> never hear my voice in the final cut, so your answer has to stand on its own.
>
> **Two:** if you flub a sentence, just stop, take a breath, and start that sentence over.
> Don't apologize, don't explain — I'll cut it and nobody will ever know. You cannot
> mess this up.
>
> **Three:** say "[ASP]" instead of "you guys" or "y'all." Same reason — I'm not in the
> shot, so "you guys" doesn't mean anything to a stranger.
>
> Sound good? Great. Let's go.

That third rule is the single highest-leverage thing in this document. Half of the
unusable testimonial footage in the world is people saying "you guys were great."

## 4. The question script

Each block lists the questions, then the **probe** you use when the first answer is mushy.
Ask the probes ruthlessly — the first answer is almost never the usable one.

### Block 0 — ID stamp (2 min)

1. "Say your name, your title, and your company."
2. "Now give me that as one sentence — 'I'm [Name], I run [Company] in [City].'"
3. "In one sentence: what does [Company] do, and who do you do it for?"

> **Why:** lower-third text, and a cold-open frame. Get a clean take of #2 even if
> you have to run it three times. It's five seconds of work that saves you a graphic.

### Block 1 — The Before (10 min) → hook fuel

4. "Take me back to [month/year], before you switched to [ASP]. What did a typical
   week look like?"
5. "What specifically wasn't working?"
6. "What was that actually costing you — in money, in hours, or in stress?"
7. "Was there a specific moment where you thought, 'this can't keep going like this'?"
8. "What had you already tried before [ASP] that didn't fix it?"

> **Probes:** "Give me that with a number." · "Say that again, but start with 'Before [ASP]…'" ·
> "What did that feel like?" · [silence — count to four; they'll fill it with the good version]
>
> **Why:** Q7 is your #1 hook. A specific moment beats a general complaint every time —
> "I remember sitting in my truck at 9pm doing quotes on my phone" outperforms
> "we were disorganized" by a mile.

### Block 2 — The Hesitation (7 min) → objection-handling ad

9. "When switching to [ASP] first came up, what was your honest gut reaction?"
10. "What were you skeptical about?"
11. "What almost stopped you from doing it?"
12. "What was the downside if it didn't work?"
13. "So what made you say yes anyway?"

> **Probes:** "Be blunt — what did you think was going to go wrong?" ·
> "Had you been burned before?"
>
> **Why:** this block alone is an entire ad. Your cold audience is sitting on the exact
> objection they name, and hearing a peer name it and then dismiss it does more work
> than any claim you can make yourself.

### Block 3 — The Switch (7 min) → risk reversal

14. "Walk me through the first couple of weeks on [ASP]. What surprised you?"
15. "How much work was it on your end? What did you expect versus what it actually was?"
16. "How long before you saw the first sign it was working?"
17. "Was there a point where it clicked?"

> **Probes:** "Give me the 'I expected X, it was actually Y' version."
>
> **Why:** switching cost is the last objection before the buy. Q15 in the
> "I thought it'd take a month, it took a week" form is a closer.

### Block 4 — The Numbers (10 min) → 4:5 stat pops

18. "What's the number that's changed the most since the switch?"
19. "Give me that as a before-and-after: 'We went from X to Y in Z.'"
20. "Where was your [cost per lead / CPA / close rate] before, and where is it now?"
21. "What did that do downstream — [revenue / booked jobs / pipeline]?"
22. "What number surprised you the most?"
23. "If you had to convince one skeptic with a single number, which one would you use?"

> **Probes:** "Say the whole thing in one sentence with both numbers in it." ·
> "Round it if you want — is it fair to say roughly [X]?" ·
> "Can I use that figure publicly?" ← **ask this out loud, on the recording, for every stat.**
>
> **Why:** Q19's forced sentence structure is what makes a stat animate cleanly. You need
> "We went from 40 leads a month to 140" as one continuous clause — if the before and
> after live in separate sentences 90 seconds apart, you can't cut it.

### Block 5 — The Payoff (6 min) → the line that carries the ad

24. "What can you do now that you couldn't do a year ago?"
25. "What's changed about how you actually run the business day to day?"
26. "Has anything changed outside of work?"
27. "How does Sunday night feel now compared to then?"

> **Probes:** [silence] · "Say more about that."
>
> **Why:** Q26 and Q27 produce the line people screenshot. Numbers earn the click;
> this earns the trust. Do not skip these because you already got good stats.

### Block 6 — Recommendation (5 min) → CTA and qualification

28. "Who is [ASP] absolutely right for?"
29. "Who is it *not* right for?"
30. "What would you say to a [ICP] who's sitting exactly where you were a year ago?"
31. "If a friend in your industry asked whether they should do this, what do you tell them?"

> **Why:** Q29 is counterintuitive and worth the airtime. A testimonial that names who
> shouldn't buy reads as honest, and it pre-qualifies your leads for free.

### Block 7 — Soundbite harvest (5 min) → the ad gold

Frame it explicitly: *"Last stretch — these are quick-fire, I'm just fishing for one-liners.
Short answers, and feel free to give me two or three versions of each."*

32. "Finish this sentence: 'Before [ASP], I was ___. Now I'm ___.'"
33. "Finish this: 'The thing nobody tells you about [core problem] is ___.'"
34. "Finish this: 'If I could go back, I'd have done this ___.'"
35. "Give me the whole story in ten seconds — like you're telling a friend at a bar."
36. "One sentence, billboard-sized. What is it?"
37. "Look straight into the camera and tell [ICP] what to do."

> **Probes:** "Great — give me one more version, shorter." · "Same thing, but angrier." ·
> "Same thing, but like you're recommending it, not selling it."
>
> **Why:** you're not interviewing anymore, you're casting. Ask for three takes of #36
> and #37 and pick in the edit. Q37 is your ad closer — with them full-frame in 9:16,
> talking directly to the viewer.

## 5. Silent captures — do not end the call without these

These take four minutes and they're the difference between a split-screen that works
and one you have to fake.

- [ ] **Client, 15 sec of relaxed silence** — small smile, slight nod, looking at camera.
      This is your bed for stat overlays in the 4:5.
- [ ] **You, 20 sec of silent listening** — nodding, reacting, occasional smile, *not*
      talking. This fills your bottom pane whenever the client is mid-answer and you'd
      otherwise be a frozen face or a mouth-open frame.
- [ ] **You, 10 sec of "asking" body language** — leaning in slightly, as if mid-question.
- [ ] **Client says their company name and city clearly**, twice.
- [ ] **30 sec room tone** on both ends.

Also collect, in the chat or by email:

- [ ] Logo (SVG or transparent PNG)
- [ ] 3–5 photos of their work / team / storefront for cutaways
- [ ] Screenshot of the dashboard or report behind their headline stat
- [ ] Written consent (below)

## 6. Consent — get it on the recording *and* in writing

Say on camera, at the end:

> "Last thing, for the file — can you confirm you're OK with us using this
> recording and the numbers you shared in our marketing, including paid ads
> on Meta and other platforms?"

And send a short written release the same day. Verbal consent on the recording is your
fast proof; the written release is what actually protects you. Include: usage in paid and
organic media, right to edit for length, territory, term, and a named contact to revoke.

**Ad compliance notes for Meta:**

- Only claim figures the client confirmed and you can substantiate. If you'd struggle
  to produce the receipt, don't put it on screen.
- Avoid "guaranteed," "risk-free," and anything that reads as a promise of specific
  results for the viewer.
- Where you show a strong result, run a small persistent super: **"Individual results
  vary."** Cheap insurance, near-zero effect on performance.
- Avoid second-person phrasing that implies you know something about the viewer
  ("struggling with your leads?"). Meta's personal-attributes rules bite on framing that
  asserts something about the individual seeing the ad — write about the *client's*
  experience, not the viewer's condition.
- Re-verify current policy before launch; this is guidance, not legal advice.

## 7. Edit map — which answer becomes which asset

Cut each testimonial into this set. One 50-minute call should yield all of it.

### A. 9:16 vertical split (1080×1920) — 25–40 sec

- **Layout:** client 0–1050px, you 1050–1920px. Keep your face in the 1100–1400 band.
- **Cold open (0:00–0:03):** the strongest fragment from Q7 (the breaking-point moment)
  or Q9 (the skeptical gut reaction). Start mid-sentence. No intro, no logo, no name card.
- **0:03–0:08:** the pain, from Q5/Q6.
- **0:08–0:20:** the turn — Q13 or Q17.
- **0:20–0:32:** the number, from Q19. Overlay the figure in the **top pane only**,
  tucked under the client's chin.
- **0:32–0:40:** Q31 or Q37 as the close.
- **Captions:** burned in, positioned in the **top pane**, y≈800–950. Never in your pane —
  the platform's own caption row lives down there.
- **Your bottom pane:** silent reaction footage from the capture list, plus one or two
  moments where you actually ask a question aloud. It reads as a conversation, which is
  the entire point of the format — it's a podcast clip, not an ad, and it gets treated
  like one in the feed.

### B. 4:5 stat card (1080×1350) — 20–30 sec

- **Layout:** client full-frame, positioned screen-left. Right third reserved for overlays.
- Answers from Block 4 only. Each stat animates in on the beat where they say the number
  and holds ~2.5 sec.
- Use the 15 sec of silent smiling footage to hold the frame when a stat needs longer than
  the audio gives you.
- Best performer as a **retargeting** asset — it's proof, not story, so it works on people
  who already know the offer.

### C. 9:16 full-frame quote (1080×1920) — 10–15 sec

- One answer, unbroken. Q32, Q33, Q35, or Q36.
- Client fills the frame, large burned captions in the center-safe band (y≈300–1450).
- The cheapest asset to make and often the best top-of-funnel hook.

### D. 6-second cutdown (9:16)

- Q36 (the billboard line) alone, no setup.
- Frequency-capped reminder / sequenced second-touch.

### E. Website (16:9)

- The full 90–120 second narrative arc: Before → Hesitation → Switch → Numbers → Payoff.
- This is the only place the long version belongs. Do not try to run it as an ad.

## 8. Testing structure on Meta

Per client testimonial, ship into a single ad set:

- **3 hook variants of asset A** — same body, three different opening 3 seconds
  (breaking-point moment / skeptical reaction / the number stated cold).
- **1× asset B** (4:5 stats)
- **1× asset C** (full-frame quote)

Hook variants belong at the **ad** level inside one ad set, not split across ad sets —
you want them competing for the same delivery, and you want the learning phase to
concentrate rather than fragment across duplicated audiences.

With multiple clients recorded, resist the urge to run all of them at once. Get one
testimonial to a stable read first; the winner's structure tells you which questions
to lean on hardest in the next call, and this script tightens with every round.
